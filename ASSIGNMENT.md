# CS6140 Final Project Overview

## Objective

The final project is intended to give you a chance to apply your machine learning skills and "push" yourself as far as you can go in the time available. You should choose a project that interests you, and that will allow you to demonstrate what you have learned in the course. Each final project must have the following formal components:

- A clear description of the problem you are trying to solve, using formal language to define the inputs and outputs.
- A formal definition of the input and output datasets, including details like distribution, size, missingness, and any preprocessing steps.
- A formal definition and motivation for the evaluation metrics you will use to assess your model's performance.
- A description of the model(s) you will use, including any relevant mathematical formulations.
    - You MUST use at least one simple model (defined as something you can train with scikit-learn) as a baseline.
    - You MUST use one more complex model (either because it is a deep learning model, or because it uses advanced techniques like ensembling, transfer learning, etc.)
- A description of your training and validation procedure, including any hyperparameter tuning methods.
- A discussion of potential pitfalls, ethical considerations, and limitations of your approach.

**Your final project should be of a quality that could be the cornerstone of your professional portfolio. The best projects will be of a quality that could be submitted to a conference.**

## Individual Proposal (5%)

A brief one-page overview of your ideas for your final project. This proposal should include the following elements:

- Project title
- Problem statement and motivation
- Dataset description
- Baseline model idea
- More advanced model idea
- Evaluation metrics
- Planned timeline over the last 2-3 weeks of the class
- Areas you are uncertain about or would like assistance from the Professor or TA

I would recommend writing this initial proposal in Google Docs or OneDrive so that you can easily share it with me, the TA, or your classmates for feedback.

If you already have a project group, you *may* discuss your answers with your group members, but each student must submit their own individual proposal. In particular, the "baseline model idea" and "more advanced model idea" sections should reflect your own thoughts and arguments for why they would work, even if they differ from your group members.

## Group Formation and Updated Proposal (5%)

After the spring break week, you will submit an updated proposal that reflects your finalized project plan and your group members (if applicable). This updated proposal should include:
- All elements from the individual proposal, revised as needed
- Names and roles of all group members (if applicable)
  - By "roles", I mean who is responsible for what aspects of the project (e.g., data preprocessing, modeling, evaluation, writing the report, etc.)
- A brief plan for how you will coordinate and communicate as a group (if applicable)

This updated proposal should be no more than two pages in length and should be submitted as a PDF file. If you are new to LaTeX or Overleaf, this might be a good time to start familiarizing yourself with it, as the final report will need to be written in LaTeX.

## Project Code (10%)

You must submit all code used for your final project, including data preprocessing, model training, and evaluation. Your code should be well-documented and organized in a way that allows others to understand and reproduce your results. You may use any framework you like, but libraries such as scikit-learn, TensorFlow, or PyTorch is recommended.

You are encouraged to use AI asistance to help you organize and document your code, but you must ensure that the final code is your own work and that you understand it fully. You must also include a README file that explains how to run your code and any dependencies required.

## Final Presentation (30%)

Your final presentation should be a five-minute summary of your work in the format of a "lightning talk" or a pecha kucha. You have two options for delivering your final presentation:

### Option A: Prerecorded Presentation

You must record a five-minute presentation summarizing your project. The exact runtime of your video must be between 4:30 and 5:30. You may use any presentation software you like (PowerPoint, Google Slides, etc.) to create your slides, but you must record a video of yourself or your group presenting the slides. You may use any screen recording software you like (e.g., OBS Studio, Zoom, Loom, etc.) to record your presentation.

In lieu of live Q&A, you will be required to post your presentation in the course discussion forum, and respond to at least three questions or comments from your classmates or the instructors.

### Option B: Live Presentation

You will give a five-minute live presentation summarizing your project during our scheduled class time on 4/22. You may use any presentation software you like (PowerPoint, Google Slides, etc.) to create your slides. You will have five minutes to present, followed by a brief Q&A session.

### Q&A and Discussion Participation (5%)

During the final presentations, you are expected to write a comment on at least one recorded presentation and ask at least one question during the Q&A session. You will receive participation points for engaging in the discussion and providing thoughtful feedback or questions.

## Final Written Report (30%)

Your final written report should be in the format of a conference paper, following the ICML 2026 template. You must use Overleaf to write your report (and we will provide a tutorial on how to use Overleaf if you are not familiar with it). Further details will be provided after the midterm.
